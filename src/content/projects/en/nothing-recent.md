A speculative HCI prototype about compulsory hijab, enforcement in public space, and what an interface can honestly promise.

In Iran, compulsory hijab is enforced in the street by patrols. Apps that share their whereabouts are not hypothetical: Gershad crowdsourced them on a map in 2016. *Nothing Recent* takes that familiar form — reported sightings, nearby, minutes old — and designs against its central promise.

> How might an interface designed for protection acknowledge, in its own form, that it cannot protect the person using it?

The app opens as ROWS, a stitch counter that genuinely counts rows. A long press on the number opens the inside. Every sighting dies after forty minutes, fading the whole way, and when they are gone the screen says so — and says that this is not the same as the street being clear.

It is an exploratory design artefact, not a validated intervention and not a safety tool. Every sighting in the prototype is invented: there is no location, no account and no server, nothing is stored and nothing is sent. It does not speak for women living in Iran; it is a design question asked from my own position, as an Iranian designer working in Germany.

The full project page, with the research question, the situated context and the critical questions, is at [mahyamirzaee.github.io/nothing-recent](https://mahyamirzaee.github.io/nothing-recent/).

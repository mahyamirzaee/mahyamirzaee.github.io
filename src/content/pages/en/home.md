I am a designer and HCI researcher with roots in textile design and hands-on making. I hold an M.Sc. in Human-Computer Interaction from the University of Siegen (2026) and a B.A. in Textile Design from the Art University of Tehran. I am interested in how technology can *mediate the ways people connect*, and I explore this through making: woven objects, prototypes, and speculative design. I work in Persian and English, and speak German and Turkish at B1 level.

My strength lies in combining qualitative research with design that you can see and touch. For more than two years, I worked as a student research assistant in the research projects CrossComITS, NEBULA, and Digital Publics at the University of Siegen. I facilitated workshops and Smartphone Café sessions with older adults, coordinated concept testing, observed how people use digital tools, and analysed interview and observation data. This work led to co-authored papers at CHI 2025 (Extended Abstracts) and PDC 2026. In my master's thesis, I brought these methods together with design fiction and a clickable prototype. In a UI/UX internship at Robofinder (2026), I used the same approach in industry, turning user interviews into a UX roadmap and building high-fidelity prototypes. I move between interviews and thematic analysis, wireframes, AI-supported prototyping with Claude Code and Lovable, and physical computing, as in Shared Threads, a hand-woven textile that responds to shared touch.

My foundation is design practice. From 2016 to 2021, I ran my own studio in Tehran, Maya Studio, and took commissioned branding, textile, and visual design projects from the first concept to production, working closely with clients to understand what they needed. Running a studio taught me to look at materials, spaces, and emotions with a creative and critical eye, and to carry an idea all the way into a real object. This is the sensibility I bring into HCI.
